# Abstract

This abstract summarizes the poster with the best information available to the authors. It updates the abstract accepted by the 71º Congresso Brasileiro de Genética (Genética 2026, Trabalho #244), which cannot be changed after submission.

## Evolution of the repeatome and satellitome in three endangered insular species of the genus *Bothrops* (Squamata: Serpentes: Viperidae)

Igor Salles de Oliveira<sup>1,3</sup>, Giselle Pessanha Pessoa<sup>1,4</sup>, Giovanna Yumi Scorsim Omura<sup>5</sup>, Denis Jacob Machado<sup>5,6</sup>, Milton Yutaka Nishiyama Junior<sup>2</sup>, and Maria José de Jesus Silva<sup>1,3</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Laboratório de Ecologia e Evolução, Instituto Butantan, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>2</sup>Laboratório de Toxinologia Aplicada, Instituto Butantan, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>3</sup>Instituto de Biociências, Universidade de São Paulo, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>4</sup>Instituto de Ciências Biomédicas, Universidade de São Paulo, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>5</sup>Department of Bioinformatics and Genomics, University of North Carolina at Charlotte, Charlotte, NC, USA. <sup>6</sup>Center for Computational Intelligence for Predicting Health and Environmental Risks (CIPHER), University of North Carolina at Charlotte, Charlotte, NC, USA.

Island populations are small and isolated, so drift and selection can reshape their genomes quickly, and repetitive DNA is among the fastest-changing parts of a genome. The *Bothrops jararaca* group offers a natural test. The widespread mainland species *B. jararaca* has close relatives on continental islands off southeastern Brazil, cut off by rising sea levels within the last 11,000 to 55,000 years. We assembled the first genomes of three of these island endemics (*B. alcatraz*, *B. insularis*, and *B. sazimai*) and compared their transposable elements (TEs) and satellite DNA with those of *B. jararaca*. We sequenced blood DNA from one captive adult female of each species on an Oxford Nanopore PromethION, assembled the reads with Flye, and scaffolded them with LongStitch. We annotated TEs with EarlGrey and satellite DNA with TRASH in all four genomes, including the published *B. jararaca* assembly. The three assemblies have scaffold N50 of 20.88 to 31.28 Mbp, L50 of 14 to 23, and BUSCO completeness near 96%. Repeats cover 48.6 to 50.0% of each genome, and LINEs alone cover 23 to 24%. The TE landscapes of the four species overlap, and every major TE burst predates island isolation by millions of years. Below a Kimura 2-parameter distance of 5%, TcMar-MITE and Jockey elements rise toward the present in all four species, while Gypsy, nMITE, and unclassified DNA elements rise only in the island species. We found 1,720 satellite DNA families in 1,347 homology groups. Most families (1,221, 71%) occur in a single species, while the 237 families shared by all four species carry the most arrays per family (Kruskal-Wallis, p < 0.001). Isolation therefore did not trigger a genome-wide repeat expansion. Instead, a few TE superfamilies show renewed activity, some only in the island species, and a small ancestral set of satellites persists beside a large species-specific satellitome. Functional studies are still needed to test whether these patterns affect adaptive traits.
